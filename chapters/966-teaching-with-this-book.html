Appendix I — Teaching with This Book

This appendix is for the instructor. It lists what comes with each chapter beyond the text, and it lays out three fourteen-week course plans that draw on the same twenty-eight chapters at different depths: an introductory bachelor’s course, a general master’s course, and an advanced master’s course that assumes the general one. The chapters were written to be teachable in one session each, and the plans are built on that unit.

I.1 What Comes with Each Chapter

Every numbered chapter carries a banner under its title with four things.

  • The code. One R script with the chapter’s code in the order it runs in the book, each chunk under the label the book uses, so that knitr::read_chunk() can pick a chunk out by name. Chunks the book shows but does not run are commented out. The script runs from the folder that holds it, beside _common.R and the tables the chapters read; a setup script installs the packages, pinned where the book pins them (Appendix D). One archive holds the scripts of all chapters.
  • The exercises as a file. The chapter’s exercises as comments, each followed by room to work, after the chapter’s data setup so that the student starts from the same objects the chapter uses.
  • The solutions. The worked solutions are Appendix G, computed at render time on the same data; their code is downloadable beside the exercises. An instructor who prefers to hand the solutions out later can point students at the exercise file alone.
  • Slides, where a deck exists. The decks are built from the chapter’s own code and figures, so a figure on a slide is the figure in the book.

The chapter’s review status sits in the same banner, and the text can be annotated in place; Section J.2 and the repository’s REVIEWING.md say how that works.

I.2 Three Course Plans

The plans assume fourteen weeks of one session each, with the chapter read before the session and the exercises worked after it. They differ in what they assume and in what they leave out, not in how a chapter is taught.

I.2.1 An introductory course

For a bachelor’s course in portfolio management that follows an introductory finance course. One chapter a week, the central chapter over two weeks, and nothing from Parts V, VI or VIII: the course ends where a portfolio has been built, constrained, tested and costed. The R refresher of Appendix E is read in the first week.

Estimation error comes after the optimizer rather than before it, so that the student has seen what the optimizer does with a sample moment before learning how little the sample moment is worth; risk-based allocation then reads as the answer to that problem rather than as a separate topic.

I.2.2 A general master’s course

For a master’s course in asset allocation that follows a bachelor’s course in investments. Parts I to IV and VII in full, one chapter from Part V, one sleeve from Part VI, and a week left for the instructor’s choice. Two chapters in a week where one of them is short.

The chapters left out, 18, 20, 22 and 26 to 28, are the advanced course.

I.2.3 An advanced master’s course

For a second master’s course that assumes the general one: Parts V, VI and VIII in full, the scenario-analysis appendix, and a project. The first week reopens the three chapters the rest depend on, read again rather than taught again. Every exercise of a chapter is assigned, because at this level the exercises are where the chapter is learned.

A project that fits the course is a mandate: a client, a set of constraints, a policy, and a backtest that reports what the policy cost and where it failed. The chapters of Part VII are the rubric.
