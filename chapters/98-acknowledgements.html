Appendix K — Acknowledgements

Draft|To comment, select a passage and annotate it; see how reviews work.

This book has a long prehistory. Earlier versions were written between 2018 and 2024 under the title Tidy Portfoliomanagement in R, and substantial parts of the present material began there, in a different voice and a different structure. The people who wrote with me then are the reason the present text could be built as fast as it was.

Elizabet Sanyal wrote the first working versions of the covariance, expected-return and fully flexible views material, and her critique of the backtesting chapter shaped how this edition separates demonstration from diagnosis. Dominik Brändle contributed the original backtesting and optimization code. Lukas Salcher and Merlin Bartel contributed to the earlier chapters, and their research with me on estimation error, on the economic value of forecast accuracy and on diversifying estimation errors underlies Parts III and VIII.

The developers of PortfolioAnalytics and PerformanceAnalytics, in particular Brian G. Peterson and Peter Carl, have maintained the machinery this book is about for the better part of two decades. Where a chapter records a default that fails or a constraint that is dropped, it does so with respect for a package whose ambition is the reason such things can be found at all, and every finding of that kind has been reported upstream. Ross Bennett’s work on the package’s second-generation interface is what the chapters call.

Christoph Scheuch, Stefan Voigt and Patrick Weiss, and Guillaume Coqueret and Tony Guida, wrote the two books beside which this one is meant to sit. Their example settled most of the questions about how a book of this kind should be built, and the reader who wants the data acquisition or the machine learning that this book cites rather than reproduces will find it in theirs.

The students of the portfolio management courses at the University of Liechtenstein have worked through earlier versions of most exercises, and their questions are the reason several of the callouts exist. The simulated market was built for their course; the caveats in Appendix A are the ones they found.

Finally, to my family, for the evenings.

J  Colophon and Changelog
